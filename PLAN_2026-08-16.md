### N0 — housekeeping · ~15 min · zero risk
Branch off PR #5's tip. Fix the stale `OWNER_DECISIONS.md` header from §0(b).

### N1 — build the real-time `A-029` endurance harness · ~2–3 h
The existing `verification/run_f5_acceptance.py` runs A-029 on a **simulated**
clock — 240 ticks, `TICK_MS = 1000`, `clock["t"]` advanced by hand. A leak or an
hour-19 exhaustion is invisible to it by construction. A new
`verification/run_a029_endurance.py` runs the identical protocol and identical
assertions against a **real** clock and a real `Supervisor`:

- **Declared cadence: 60 s** — and this is a decision the harness must *record*,
  because nothing in `contracts/` or `spec/` declares a production tick cadence
  today. `INV-AUTO-1` fixes the tolerance at `3 × tick_interval`, so 60 s gives a
  180 s gap ceiling and 1,440 heartbeats across the window. Event volume stays
  trivial and the run can share the machine with ordinary use.
- **Part 3's `T` is drawn by a separate controller process** and never enters
  the system under test — same property the compressed run has, now across a
  process boundary rather than a variable scope.
- **Sampled every tick:** RSS, open handles, `state.db` size, event-log row
  count, tick latency. These are what convert 24 elapsed hours into evidence;
  without them the long run only proves it did not crash.
- Resumable from checkpoint, so a power event costs the remainder, not the run.

### N2 — red-test the harness before it is trusted · ~1 h
The report's §5 is the reason this step is not optional: **four** controls
passed while measuring nothing, and the only defence that worked was running
each control against a deliberately broken build and requiring failure. A new
24-hour harness that has never been shown red is that failure mode with a
24-hour price tag.

Three broken builds, each at real cadence over a short window:

| Broken build | Must fail |
|---|---|
| Loop killed partway | Part 1 (liveness) |
| Action emitted with no registered `trigger_id` | Part 2 (provenance) |
| Pure cron (the C1 build) | Part 3 (anti-cron) — and **only** part 3 |

**If any control passes, the run does not launch.** The defect gets written up
instead, and you get a harness report tomorrow rather than a false green in
24 hours.

### N3 — launch the 24 h run · at whatever hour N2 clears
From a **pinned worktree at a fixed commit** with its own store directory, so
nothing I edit afterwards can perturb a run in flight. Start time, commit SHA
and the sealed `T` draws are recorded at launch.

Finishing time is ~24 h after launch — so it reports back the following
morning, not tomorrow's. Tomorrow you get its first ~6 hours of samples, which
is already enough to see a leak's slope if there is one.

### N4 — `SPIKE-008` criterion 8: the upcaster registry · rest of the night
The §0(a) finding. Build the event codec's upcaster registry in `core/`, then
re-run the spike probe for criterion 8 against it. Three assertions, from the
spike text: the reader receives the upcast form; **stored bytes are unchanged
after reading**; a **missing** upcaster raises explicitly rather than silently
dropping a field. Red-tested the same way as everything else — the
missing-upcaster path in particular, since "silently dropped field" is exactly
the failure that looks green.

This closes an F0 deferral and moves SPIKE-008 from seven criteria to eight.

### The stop rule
If N1 and N2 have not cleared by hour 5 of the night, **nothing launches**. An

## 3. The two decisions that are actually yours

Both were named in the report and neither was laid out. Here they are in full,
so you can rule without opening the corpus.

### 3.1 `OWNER-DECISION-006(c)` — the ADR namespace · ~2 minutes

**What happened.** `REM-003`, `REM-004`, `REM-005` and `REM-006` each cited a
decision record at `ADR-017`…`ADR-020`. **Those ids do not exist.** The
Foundation register defines `ADR-001`…`016` and is the authority for the
namespace, the way the COC is for SR ids. An engineering pass corrected the four
citations to point at the REM specifications instead — which already carry the
full ADR content: context, options with recorded rejections, decision,
consequences, invariants, verification. **No decision content changed**, only
where the pointer lands.

**What you are choosing.** Whether those four decisions get numbered ADR ids of
their own.

| | Option A — leave as corrected | Option B — extend the register |
|---|---|---|
| The records live in | `REM-003…006`, cited directly | new `ADR-017`…`020` |
| Cost | none, it is already done | an additive baseline change + four new files |
| Buys you | nothing further; they are enforceable today | uniform "decisions are ADRs" navigation |

**Engineering's recommendation: A.** The decisions exist and are enforced;
adding four numbers is bookkeeping. It is recorded as an option because the
preference is legitimately yours — if you expect to navigate this corpus by ADR
number in two years, B is not wrong.

**Blocks:** nothing. This is why it can wait for coffee.

### 3.2 The `ADR-001` / `ADR-012` authority question · ~10 minutes

**This one is not in the numbered decision list, and it is the more interesting
of the two.**

**What happened.** Four ADRs, two authority stories:

| ADR | Status line, verbatim | Stamped by |
|---|---|---|
| `ADR-002` persistence | ACCEPTED — **by the project owner, 2026-08-14**, under `OWNER-DECISION-006(b)`, on the seven SPIKE-008 criteria tested | **you** |
| `ADR-007` device identity | RATIFIED 2026-08-14, option A, four consequences put to the owner and accepted | **you** |

| `ADR-012` deployment topology | **ACCEPTED (bounded)** — resolved exactly far enough to give REM-004 a referent; concrete packaging remains open | Chief Architect / System Integrator role |

`ADR-002` was deliberately filed `PROPOSED` rather than self-accepted, on the
stated grounds that *"a gate discharged by its author is not a gate"* — and it
was then escalated to you and you ruled. `ADR-001` and `ADR-012` were stamped

notices the asymmetry itself, at `OWNER_DECISIONS.md` line 225: *"Either all
three are confirmed together, or the earlier two should be re-confirmed on the
same basis."*

**Why it matters more than a paperwork tidy.** `ADR-001` is the coordination
substrate — it is what puts `node_id` inside the HLC tuple, and every ordering
and supersession claim in F0 rests on it. `core/hlc.py` is described in the
report as *"the total order — nothing else may compare timestamps."* That is a
lot of load on a record whose acceptance authority is contested by its own
register.

**What you are choosing.**

| | Option A — confirm both now | Option B — re-confirm at the readiness gate | Option C — leave standing |
|---|---|---|---|
| What you do | read the two status lines and their scope claims, then rule as you did for ADR-002/007 | defer to the R-gate, where the corpus already says all three are confirmed together | accept "engineering authority" as sufficient for these two |
| Cost | ~10 min now | none now | none ever |
| Risk | none | the gate arrives with the question still open, which is where gates go soft | the asymmetry stays in the record: two ADRs the owner ruled on, two an agent stamped, no stated principle separating them |

**Engineering's recommendation: A.** Not because the two records are suspect —
their scope claims are narrow and plausibly within engineering authority — but
because the *principle* is what is missing. Ruling now costs ten minutes and
converts "an agent decided which decisions needed the owner" into "the owner
decided, and here is the line." That line is worth having written down before
