09 · Structure

The dock swap.

Inside VESPER's workspace the system Dock goes under the screen and VESPER's main menu takes its position — a long glass bar, bottom-centre. Push the cursor to the bottom edge and the system Dock rises in front of the main menu, exactly as it does inside any fullscreen app. Try it.

How the swap is actually done

Not by fighting the Dock. VESPER sets NSApplicationPresentationAutoHideDock while its window is frontmost — the same API a fullscreen app uses. macOS reverts it the instant VESPER stops being frontmost, and the cursor-to-edge reveal is the system's own behaviour, free.

Which matters for permissions: this is VESPER changing its own presentation, so it belongs in the self tier of host_actions.toml, alongside setting its own QoS class. It is not a host mutation and does not need your approval each time.

Occlusion, not evacuation

When the system Dock rises, VESPER's main menu stays exactly where it is and is covered. It does not slide away, shrink or re-flow. Two bars trading places would be a fight; one bar passing in front of another is a stack.

The main menu dims to 30% while occluded, so it is visibly still there rather than apparently gone.

The island is welded to the notch

Corrected 21 Aug  It was drawn as a pill floating under the menu bar. Your reference says otherwise, and the gap was the whole error: the island's top edge is y = 0, and every state grows downward and outward from the notch's own silhouette. What the component rests on is the illusion that the hardware got bigger — one point of air kills it.

Measured on this display rather than assumed: logical 1728 × 1117 pt, safe-area top inset 32 pt, notch x 771 → 956 — 185 pt wide, dead centre.

The shell is #000000

Not our black. #03141f is a very dark blue-teal, and welded to the true-black camera housing beside it, it would read as a lighter patch. Our black is used one step in, as the ground the contents sit on.

Dark in every theme

The housing is black in light mode because it is a physical object. This is not an exception to the adaptive rule — it is what the rule already outputs when the surrounding ground is #000. The engine needs no special case, and gets none.

Glass at the weld is edge, not blur

For the first 120 pt below the bezel the panel is opaque, so a backdrop-filter there has nothing to refract. Glass is carried by the specular edge, the hairline and the shadow alone. Below that line it becomes real Dock-tier glass.

The camera is a hole in the layout, not an obstacle to dodge. Anything in the band y ∈ [0, 32] must clear x ∈ [771, 956]; below y = 32 the full width is free. So the island is a two-row grid — a shoulder row split by a 185 pt spacer, over a body row spanning the whole width.

10 · Floating dialogs

Split, as you asked.

It was one catalogue row doing two jobs. Here is the cut: what genuinely needs to float, and what was only ever a card pretending to.

Stays a dialog

Things that interrupt because they are addressed to you and are worthless later:

Approval
a risky action waiting on you — the only kind that blocks
Clarify
VESPER cannot proceed without one answer
Alert
thermal, battery, a deadline arriving
Capture
quick input, summoned from anywhere

Becomes a card

Things you go and look at, which were only floating because they lived in the same row:

Surfaces
which messages may float, where they anchor, how long they hold, what may interrupt
Backlog
everything that floated while you were away
History
what you approved and refused, and when

The floating layer itself stays a mechanism — it has a card that configures it, not a card that is it.
