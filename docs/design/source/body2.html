02 · The adaptive rule — your "IMPORTANT"

Colour that curates itself.

You asked for colours that change on their own so they land well no matter what is behind them. That is an algorithm, not a preference, so here it is as one — five steps, running per glass surface.

1 · Sample the backdrop

Downsample whatever is behind the surface to 8×8, convert to OKLab, take mean L, mean chroma, and dominant hue with its variance. In the workspace that is the wallpaper and any window beneath; on a card it is the ambience ground.

2 · Score the eight

Only the eight chromatic colours compete — the two grounds are never accents. Score each on hue distance from the backdrop (want ≥ 40°), predicted contrast once lightness is solved, and a penalty if a sibling within 240 px already holds it.

3 · Solve lightness, keep hue

Hold H and C from the palette entry; move L only, until APCA Lc ≥ 60 for text and ≥ 45 for borders and lines against the composited glass — not against the raw backdrop. Then gamut-map chroma back into sRGB.

4 · Hysteresis — the part that is easy to miss

Re-evaluate only when backdrop mean L shifts more than 0.08 or hue more than 25°, at most once per 400 ms, and cross-fade over --smooth. Without this the whole interface shimmers as you scroll, because every frame re-picks a marginally better colour. Adaptive without damping reads as broken, not alive.

5 · Fail closed. If the backdrop cannot be sampled — no read permission, an occluded surface, a capture that timed out — the surface takes the theme's static accent and marks itself unadapted. It does not guess. That is the same rule as INV-HW-2: an unmeasured value is UNKNOWN, and UNKNOWN is never treated as a good one.

Two system settings VESPER must obey. macOS has Reduce transparency and Reduce motion, and both are exposed to the webview as media queries. Under reduced transparency every tier collapses to an opaque --veil surface; under reduced motion every spring becomes a cut. A system that claims to collaborate with the host does not override the host's accessibility choices — this page already does both, and you can test it in System Settings right now.

04 · Type

Three voices.

Body · Aeonik

Everything VESPER says plainly.

CoType Foundry. Commercial licence required. Geometric grotesque — circular bowls, tall x-height, low contrast.

Display · Canela

Headlines, and moments.

Commercial Type, Miguel Reyes. Commercial licence required. Wedge serif with flared stems and high contrast.

Hand · Learning Curve

when Vesper writes to you

Blue Vinyl Fonts. Free — you can install this one today. Connected school cursive.

None of the three are installed on this machine, and this page cannot load them. An artifact may only pull fonts from Google Fonts, so what you are reading is substitutes: Outfit for Aeonik, Instrument Serif for Canela, Dancing Script for Learning Curve. The real ones get bundled into the Tauri app as local @font-face files, which has no such restriction — Aeonik and Canela need buying first, Learning Curve does not.

The rule for the hand

A third typeface with no rule is decoration. Learning Curve is VESPER writing in the first person, unprompted — a margin note, a thing it noticed, an aside while you work, the line on a milestone. It is never used for a system message, never for a number, never for anything you asked for directly, and never for more than about two lines. If it shows up in an error dialog, the rule has been broken.
